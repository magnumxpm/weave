# Where Weave changes the day

Weave earns its place wherever commitments are made out loud and then have to survive
contact with a calendar. The pattern repeats across every function in an organisation, and
the same engine serves all of them — because what Weave models is not a job role, it is the
moment somebody says *"yes, I'll do that."*

---

## Engineering

**The standup that quietly reassigns work.** Priya is handed the migration guide, says it
should go to Sarah, and Sarah agrees — three turns, one owner change, and by Thursday nobody
is certain who has it. Weave records the hand-off with the turn that proves it, and the guide
appears in Sarah's card, not Priya's.

**Incident reviews where the follow-through is the whole point.** A review generates
reproduction steps, implementation notes, and a dozen half-assigned fixes. Weave keeps the
reproduction steps and technical detail attached to the meeting they came from, and gives
each fix to the person who actually accepted it — with the detail they need to do it still
attached.

**Dependency chains across squads.** *"We can't start the rollout until the rollback plan is
signed off."* That sentence becomes a graph edge, and the rollback plan rises to the top of
its owner's queue because three other commitments are waiting behind it.

## Product and programme management

**The status update that writes itself.** Instead of chasing eight people before a steering
call, ask Weave what is outstanding, what is blocked, and what has been carried over — and
get an answer grounded in what people actually said, with the meetings cited.

**Carry-over made undeniable.** A deliverable raised on the 12th, restated on the 19th and
carried again on the 28th is one commitment with a three-week history, not three tasks in
three tools. The pattern becomes visible at exactly the moment it becomes a risk.

**Decision archaeology.** *"What did we decide about the vendor numbers?"* is answered from
the meeting where it was decided, with the decision, the reasoning and the date.

## Leadership

**Prepared without a pre-read.** Walk into a one-to-one already knowing what you picked up
recently, what is blocking you, and what has gone quiet — from your own view of your own
work, which is the only view that exists.

**Commitments that survive reorganisations.** When ownership moves, the history moves with
it. The new owner inherits the full mention timeline rather than a one-line title with no
provenance.

## Consulting and client services

**Every client call ends with obligations in both directions.** Weave separates what *you*
committed to from what was merely discussed, and keeps the client's exact words attached, so
scope conversations start from the record rather than from memory.

**Multi-account context switching.** Six clients, twelve calls, one brain. Weave answers
per-person and per-meeting, so switching accounts costs a question rather than an evening of
re-reading notes.

## Sales and customer success

**Follow-through as a differentiator.** The promises made on a call — send the pricing sheet,
loop in the solutions architect, confirm the security questionnaire — are captured with
deadlines and surface before they age. The commitment nobody remembers making is the one that
loses the renewal.

## Support and operations

**Escalations with their reproduction steps intact.** Troubleshooting steps discussed on a
call are preserved in the meeting summary and attached to the action item, so the person who
picks it up is not starting from a one-line ticket.

**Rotations and hand-offs.** What was promised during one rotation stays visible into the
next, because commitments live with their owner and their history rather than in a channel
that scrolls.

## Distributed and asynchronous teams

**No transcript re-reading across time zones.** Someone who could not attend asks what came
out of the meeting and what they picked up, and gets a scoped, personal answer instead of a
90-minute recording.

**Fewer status meetings.** A meeting held to find out where things stand is a meeting that a
well-maintained commitment graph makes unnecessary.

## New joiners

**Context without interrupting anybody.** A new team member asks what was decided about the
migration and gets the decisions, the implementation notes, and the meetings they came from —
scoped to what they are already entitled to see.

**Their first commitments, tracked from day one.** Nobody has to teach them a tracking
system. They add Weave in Chat, keep their promises in meetings as everyone does, and Weave
keeps the ledger.

## Compliance-conscious environments

**Provenance for every claim.** Each commitment cites the meeting, the turn, and the words
spoken. Nothing is asserted that cannot be traced to a transcript.

**Read-only by construction.** Weave never writes into Drive, Tasks or Calendar, and every
read happens as the person themselves, against material they can already open — so adopting
it changes what people *know*, never what they can *reach*.

## The pattern underneath

Every one of these scenarios is the same three problems wearing different clothes:

| The problem | What Weave does |
|---|---|
| **Promises evaporate.** They are made out loud and recorded nowhere. | Captures them from the transcript, with the turn that proves it |
| **Context is scattered.** The task is here, the reasoning is in a meeting nobody re-reads. | Attaches the prior facts that still matter, from sources the owner can already see |
| **Priority is invisible.** Everything looks equally urgent until something is late. | Orders by deadline, blocking impact, staleness and carry-over — and explains why |

Solve those three once, in a way people trust, and the improvement compounds across every
team that meets.
